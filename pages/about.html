◈ About

I've worn many different hats, and you'd never believe what was in common.

I studied oboe at UC's College-Conservatory of Music. I enlisted in the Army and spent five years doing Arabic signals intelligence. I work in supply-chain planning at Procter & Gamble. I play bass in a band called Mantaray and I build things on the internet under the name skele.t0l.

Each of those required learning a new language under pressure, figuring out what context was missing, and reconstructing it into something that worked in the new environment. I didn't have a word for that process until recently. Now I do, and this site is where it lives.

The Arc
2014–2018

UC College-Conservatory of Music

Oboe performance and music education. Learned counterpoint, practiced until instinct formed, left to enlist. The training shaped how I listen and how I build things, even now.

2018–2025

U.S. Army: Cryptologic Language Analyst

Arabic signals intelligence at Fort Hood. TS-SCI clearance. Graduated near the top of a program with 75% attrition. Spent years learning that a correct translation requires the full context, not just the right words.

2024–Present

Procter & Gamble: Site Integrated Planner

Master data and production planning in Personal Health Care. The work is coordination across teams and systems where small errors cascade. I also co-direct the choir and play oboe in the concert band.

The Thread

Context, truth, instinct.

In every one of these environments, the work that mattered was the same: figure out what context was missing, understand it well enough to reconstruct it, and deliver something that preserved the truth of what was actually happening. Music taught me the method. The Army taught me the stakes. P&G taught me the pace.

I'm starting community college in August 2026, studying toward organizational psychology. The long-term interest is in how systems can be designed to protect the people inside them.
